1. Base Exposure Determination (Dmax)

Step-wedge calibration requires locking in a single Base Exposure Time first—the shortest exposure at which clear OHP transparency film produces maximum paper black (Dmax).

Method A: Clear Film Test Strip (Recommended)

  1. Place a strip of unprinted clear OHP film over photographic paper so half of the paper is bare and half is covered by clear film.
  2. Expose in progressive intervals (e.g. 4s, 6s, 8s, 12s, 16s, 22s) with a Grade 2 filter in the enlarger.
  3. After processing and drying, identify the shortest exposure time where the film-covered region merges indistinguishably into the bare-paper black. Lock this time, aperture, and enlarger height.

2. Reading the Wedge & Multigrade Filtration

Single Flat Exposure & Reading Reference Rails

  1. Contact-print the uncalibrated 21-step wedge ink-side down against the paper emulsion for a single uniform exposure at your Base Exposure Time.
  2. Black Point Step (Dmax): Compare steps 0, 1, 2... against the continuous 0% Ink Top Reference Rail. Enter the highest step number that remains indistinguishable from pure black (e.g. 2.0).
  3. White Point Step (Dmin): Compare the upper steps against the 100% Ink Bottom Reference Rail or unexposed paper border. Enter the lowest step number that reaches pure paper base white (e.g. 17.0).
  4. Midtone Step: Optionally enter the step number that visually represents 50% middle gray (e.g. 9.5).

Multigrade Filter Selection

  • Standard Baseline (Grade 2): Keep a Grade 2 filter in the enlarger for both step-wedge calibration and final prints. Because the digital LUT linearizes paper contrast, you do not swap filters when printing calibrated negatives.
  • Avoid Unfiltered Bulb Light with Colorized Inks: Variable-contrast emulsion combines blue-sensitive (high contrast) and green-sensitive (low contrast) halides. Under unfiltered white tungsten light, the yellow dye in pyro_amber acts like a low-contrast yellow filter in midtones. Using a Grade 2 filter fixes the blue/green spectral ratio.
  • Insufficient Highlight Opacity (Step 20 Still Gray): If Step 20 (100% ink) does not reach paper white at Grade 2, move up to a Grade 3 or 3.5 filter and repeat the wedge test. Higher grades increase blue light, where pyro_amber has maximum UV/blue optical density.
